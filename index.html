The Solar System

The Solar System is a planetary system held together by the Sun's gravity. It is located in one of the spiral arms of the Milky Way galaxy and formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago.

Planets of the solar system.

It is our home and the only known planet to harbor life. Its surface is 70% covered by liquid water and it has an atmosphere rich in nitrogen and oxygen, which helps regulate the temperature and protects us from solar radiation.

It is the gas giant and the largest planet in the solar system (more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it). It has no solid surface and is famous for its Great Red Spot, a giant storm that has lasted for hundreds of years. It has dozens of moons and a powerful magnetic field.

Known as the "Red Planet" due to the iron oxide present in its soil, it is a cold, desert world with a very thin atmosphere. It is home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system, and there is evidence that it once had liquid water on its surface.

It is the second largest planet and is distinguished by its spectacular ring system, made up of chunks of ice and rock. Like Jupiter, it is a gas giant composed primarily of hydrogen and helium, and is so low in density that, if there were a large enough ocean, Saturn would float in it.
